Common Ground

The bank and the camera ask the same four things of you. One taught them as policy; the other made them a habit.

i

Listen before proposing

AdvisoryYou do not open with a product. You ask what the year actually looks like — the move, the business, the parent who needs care — and only then does the right structure become obvious.

PracticeYou do not open with a lens. You ask what the picture is for, who will see it, and what the person is worried about — and only then does the right frame become obvious.

Both go wrong in exactly the same way: when the answer arrives before the question does.

ii

Give options, then a recommendation

AdvisoryA client is owed more than one path — and a clear view on which one you would take. Name the trade-off before they find it themselves.

PracticeThree approaches to a portrait, three ways to lay out a page. Show the range, say what each one costs, then say which one you would choose.

Presenting choices is service. Withholding a recommendation is not neutrality — it is leaving the decision heavier than you found it.

iii

Treat discretion as the default

AdvisoryAccount records, medical cancellations and appeal files are handed over on trust and under policy. Nothing leaves the desk, and nothing is discussed where it can be overheard.

PracticeA photograph of someone's face is the same kind of object. Consent is asked for, not assumed; files are stored, not scattered; and the frame that should not exist is simply not taken.

Knowing what not to leave on a desk and knowing when not to press the shutter are the same instinct.

iv

Hold the room steady

AdvisoryPeople arrive at a counter or an appeal at their least comfortable — embarrassed about money, anxious about a decision. Half the work is lowering the temperature before anything else can happen.

PracticePeople arrive in front of a camera the same way. The picture only opens up once they stop performing, and that is a matter of manner, not equipment.

In both, the technical part is the easy part. The person is the work.

And both are unforgiving of the same failure: a misfiled document and a missed frame cost the same thing — you cannot go back for either. Preparation, checking, and a tolerance for doing the unglamorous part properly are not two disciplines. They are one discipline, practised twice.
